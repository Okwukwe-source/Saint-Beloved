Every beginning is a kind of violence against silence.

To write is to insist — on meaning, on presence, on the audacity of a self that believes its inner world is worth the attention of another. This is the first word. Not the one typed into a blank page, but the one spoken quietly within, the word that says: *I have something to say.*

## On the Weight of Starting

There is a particular quality to the space before writing begins. It is not emptiness. It is fullness held in suspension — ideas that have not yet chosen their shape, feelings that have not yet found their grammar.

> The first sentence of anything is always a lie. It pretends to be a beginning when really it is already the middle of something long in motion.

What we call a beginning is really an arrival. We arrive at the page carrying everything we have been, every conversation that shaped us, every silence we have survived. The essay does not start with a title or a paragraph. It starts somewhere much earlier, in a moment we may never be able to name.

This is the first word, then. Not an answer, but a door.
