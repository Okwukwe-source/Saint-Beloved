Materialistic thinking kills enchantment. A mind that sees the world as only atoms, particles, objects, names, and the plane of matter and doesn't recognize spirits, forms, namelessness, mysteries, and an astral plane that makes the mind bend in humility and glow in wonder at terrifying beauties that may be is drab. C. S. Lewis said that the god of agriculture dies when chemical agriculture arises (The Abolition of Man). He noted that in order for science to explain phenomena, it has to reduce its mysteriousness, its enchantment, to a kind of mentally portable artifice that is explainable. Thus, a star isn't an angel, or an ethereal manifestation of supernatural reality, but only a bright ball of hydrogen and helium and whatnot.

I had a vision, but in modern parlance, we'd call it a dream, because visions seem too transcendent to be real; but transcendent things become drab because of our lack of faith and imagination. When the Apostle Paul spoke about being taken up to the third heaven (2 Cor. 12:2–4), what he experienced may, perhaps, have been what we'd call an ordinary dream (a misnomer because dreams in their very essence are a sublime fact of existence). But because he was mystically minded, not degenerated by materialistic vocabulary, his experience was enchanted and took on great significance for him. I want to be an imitator of Paul, he being an imitator of the Logos. Hence, I'll speak of my vision and not my dream as it would typically be valued.

I woke up early, around 5 a.m. It was still dark. After I prayed for about 23 minutes, it became lighter outside, but the world was hued in grey. I lay back down and I slept partially, for I was asleep but was conscious of that sleep, in a kind of liminal space, and in that space, phantasms were conjured up in my half-consciousness.

Images of the Aladdin animation I watched when I was little came to my mind. I remembered the "Open Sesame" proclamation that led a portion of the waters or rocks to open up to reveal treasures which the pirate gang took. My mind may have distorted other images of the real animation I watched. But I remember the lamp, its perceived uselessness, and its being the only treasure that Aladdin got. In my mind, I became Aladdin, and the world was no longer the Arabic fantasy but my room. I had a gnawing feeling that my friends had become billionaires with the treasures they gained, while I had only the lamp. Out of the lamp came the genie, and it asked me to make a wish. At that I almost wished to be the richest man henceforth, but I remembered the one thing needful: for God to be all in me. So I asked for that instead. It granted my wish, and I became full of God.

And that's when the vision unravelled. The implications of being full of God were understandable to me. I became the ruler of all reality. There was nothing that was impossible for me to do, except to be contrary to my nature—which was all-powerful, all-knowing, all-present. It was wonderful, and I understood the words of Paul. I understood the implications of God's being all in all (1 Cor. 15:28), of being in heaven, of our judging the angels (1 Cor. 6:3). In heaven, we would be rulers like God, mastering all of reality.

And then, a question that I had wrestled with the day before was answered for me with more potency than I had answered it before. Why were there more persons or acts of supernatural potency in the early church than today? 

Later, I wondered whether the question was based on a correct assumption, on the assumption that there was more supernatural reality in the early church and that it waned as the church progressed in time. I wonder whether there aren't arcane places in the world where mysticality prevails; whether there aren't assemblies of God who experience supernatural reality as potent as that of the early church and are hiding because exposure to the world is contamination and degeneration. However, the question of what manifests supernatural reality in great potency still stands. And the answer is the Presence of God. I think it is two things that manifest miracles—the breaking in of the spiritual plane into this mundane plane of reality we tread. They are, on the part of man, a deep consciousness of Spirit or Reality that allows the subjective consciousness to become less bound or tethered to matter, or become more possessed by Spirit or Reality; and on the part of that which is beyond us, the providence and response of Spirit or Reality.

Dreams reflect this. A dream is a domain where one is pure consciousness, not tethered or aware of any tethering (which is the same thing) to the body and matter, and, as some ancients understood, a domain where the dreamer receives communication with spirits. Here, the mind and the Spirit are One. Miracles are done when an agent is possessed by the miraculous, by a transcendent reality; by Oneness with it. If the yearning of this mundane plane is to be filled with the Presence of God, Oneness with God is our end. The dream is a fleeting stamp of a Oneness to come.
