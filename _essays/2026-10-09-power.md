While I was still on Instagram, Jeremy sent me a video of a blonde muscular guy philosophising over a concept. I don't remember the subject, and I can't find the chat with Jeremy because I've deleted my Instagram and won't return. But I remember that the account name was @h_miller76. He had some cool ideas that he'd discuss as a monologue, in a room or as he walked on the street. The most interesting of these ideas was on the concept of power. 



We know that power is the ability to cause an effect. But he added, crucially, the notion that power is the ability to cause an effect without being effected in return. He concluded that true power rests in two archetypes: the tyrant and the martyr. The tyrant injures others without being injured in return. The martyr receives injury without injuring in return, or, better put, the martyr forgives others without having the need to be forgiven himself. 



I am writing on this because I want you to be powerful. The idea of being impervious to influence–of power, is a good I want concretized in you. This could manifest itself in transcending one's circumstances and interactions, not being reactionary, but being original in agency. 



I am learning Koine Greek on an almost daily basis. I have a notebook where I jot and transcribe the original Greek of the Bible as a means of learning the language. However, due to circumstances beyond my control, I am now in a location where I can't access that notebook which is in my home. I have two options: find a means to continue studying Koine Greek, or, use the opportunity to not study until I get back home. The former option is more agentic, not allowing my work to be dictated by circumstance. The latter is more passive, yielding to happenstance. The more powerful option is the agentic one, which would require me to not allow the circumstance to dictate or alter my routine or work, and to find a means to continue what I've been doing. I've done just that by making mental transcriptions in the absence of a notebook, and it has unravelled before my cognizance the extent to which my learning thus far has brought me to my goal, and I'm quite pleased with my progress. 



The martyr archetype also shows us that power and righteousness go hand in hand. We see this in the doctrine of turning the other cheek. When you are slapped and your reaction is to punch or slap back, it is to allow the violence to effect violence in you, as an intuitive or natural course, given the ordinary reality that evil breeds evil. The normal course of human interaction is that one bad turn deserves another. If you shout at me, I shout back. If you hit me, I hit back. A bad deed towards another typically effects a bad deed in response. Turning the other cheek, blessing when being cursed, being meek in the face of violence; these are counterintuitive. To return good for evil, is to not be effected or moved by the bad behaviour of another as the ordinary course of reality would be. It is to transcend both the consciousness of the offender and the collective consciousness of people, and to create a new reality. A reality where one is an original agent for good and not a servant being influenced by the evil of another. Turning the other cheek is an act of power, and not a reaction—which by necessity is to be subject to influence, or, to be powerless. When you do good when evil is done to you, you create a paradigm where the evildoer requires your forgiveness while you need not their forgiveness. And when you forgive, you do unto the person as they cannot do unto you. You become power incarnate.
